Severity levels and incident roles

Severity matrix

LevelCriterionResponse
SEV1Full outage, money or data lossAll hands, Incident Commander, updates every 15–30 min
SEV2Major degradation for part of the usersOn-call + owning team, updates every 30–60 min
SEV3Minor impact, workaround existsTicket, business hours
SEV4No user impactBacklog

Rule: when in doubt, declare the higher severity — lowering it later is cheap.

Roles in a SEV1 or SEV2

Incident Commander

Coordinates and decides — does not debug

Ops / Tech Lead

Runs the diagnosis and the technical actions

Comms Lead

Stakeholders, support, status page

Scribe

Timeline with exact times

If they dig deeper: “Severity decides how big the response is. A SEV1 is a full outage or money or data loss: all hands, a dedicated Incident Commander and updates every 15 to 30 minutes. A SEV2 is a major degradation for part of the users. SEV3 and SEV4 become tickets. My rule is: when in doubt, declare the higher severity, because lowering it later is cheap and raising it late is expensive. In a big incident the roles are split: the Incident Commander coordinates and decides but doesn’t debug, the Ops lead does the technical work, the Comms lead talks to stakeholders, and a scribe keeps the timeline. In a small incident one person does everything — but knows they are wearing all four hats.” ES: Herramientas de on-call para nombrar: PagerDuty, Opsgenie, incident.io.
